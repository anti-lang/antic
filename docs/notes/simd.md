# Simd structs

Choices made for simd structs in each pass. They describe the inside of
the compiler. `docs/decisions.md` holds what a reader of the language or
a user of the tools can observe, under "Simd structs".

- `simd` is a contextual word, one before `struct` alone, as `packed` is. The parser sets `simd` on the item, and the checker sets it on the type before it resolves the fields, so a type that names it knows it.
- `select` may follow `.` in an expression, beside `super` and `destroy`, so that `simd.select` reads as a field of the module. It stays the keyword of the statement everywhere else.
- The checker checks a declaration once the fields are resolved. It takes one lane type, a power of two of lanes and a size of a multiple of eight bytes. It refuses `align(N)` and a bitfield. Above the vector cap of `cpu.h` it warns at the name of the struct.
- `types_mask` makes the mask of a simd struct once and keeps it on the type. It is a simd struct of `bool` with the field names of its source, in the module of its source. Its name is `mask(Vec4)`, which no declaration can spell.
- The checker writes each built-in as an `EXPR_SIMD` node. It holds the operation, the operands in the order of `enum simd_op` and the simd struct it works on. The value a built-in is called on is the first operand, a pointer to one included. A shuffle keeps the index of each lane beside them.
- `fixed_layout` of the checker gives the bytes of a type by the C rules, for `as` between a simd struct and an array or a plain struct. It refuses a type whose width a target decides, a bitfield and a symbolic array length.
- Lowering gives a simd struct an aggregate of the IR with `simd` set, and every operation a slot of its own for the result. An operation below the cap is one simd instruction of the IR. An operation on `f16` lanes, an integer division and a shift become scalar operations per lane. So does every operation on a simd struct above the cap, in a loop over the lanes.
- The six simd instructions of the IR work on values in memory, as `memcopy` does. `of` names the simd struct, `type` the lane and `field` the operation of one lane, an `ir_op`. A comparison writes one byte of 0 or 1 per lane of the mask. `vselect` takes its second value in `args[0]`.
- `fold_lanes` of lowering writes a sum, a least, a greatest and a dot of `f16` lanes or of a simd struct above the cap. The lanes go to a scratch array of the type the operation computes in, as products for a dot. Each half folds in a loop.
- The layout gives a simd struct the alignment of its size or sixteen, whichever is less. It marks one of 16 bytes as a vector, which every convention passes where C passes its vector type.
- `src/expand.c` writes one scalar operation per lane for every simd operation that the target has no instruction for. It runs after the layout, so it reads the offset of each lane. A choice between two lanes is a mask over the bits of the lane, as a saturating operation is. The least and the greatest of float lanes keep the offset of the lane they choose and read the lane again. The IR moves no float into an integer.
- `vector_native` of each target says which operations it selects at a level. The expansion covers the rest. Those are an unsigned comparison and a product of 8-bit or 64-bit lanes on x86_64. On ARM64 they are a product of 64-bit lanes and the least and greatest of them. Both leave a shuffle of more than one register, and every value whose register holds one lane.
- ARM64 works on registers of 16 bytes, or of 8 for a simd struct of 8. A vector operand carries its arrangement in its value, which the printer writes as `v16.4s`, and an element as `v16.s[0]`. The registers are v16 and v17, which allocation never gives out, and v18 to v23, which the instructions name. No value of the allocator lives in them across an operation.
- x86_64 works on registers of 16 bytes, and of 32 at x86-64-v3, whose AVX2 gives the wide forms. The printer writes the VEX form of every vector instruction at that level. It names the destination twice where the instruction reads and writes it. A 256-bit register carries a value of 1 in its operand, which the printer writes as `%ymm14`. The registers are the two of the spills and the four below them.
- A comparison narrows its lanes of all ones to the bytes of the mask. ARM64 takes `xtn` and a shift, and x86_64 the packs, where the upper half of a wide register comes down first. A choice widens the mask back with `sxtl` or the unpacks. It folds with `bsl` or with and, andn and or.
- A fold reads the registers of a value in the order that halves them, the upper half onto the lower half. It then halves the lanes of the one left with `ext` or a byte shift. The recursion holds one register per level, so a value of 16 registers takes five and the one a choice uses.
- The tests: `program_simd` runs every operation on ARM64 and, through Rosetta, on x86_64 at v1, in release mode and in dev mode. `program_abi_simd` calls C functions that take and return the vector types. `clib_simd` calls an Anti library from C through the header. `simd_width` counts the instructions of one add of 32 bytes per target and level. `program_simd_big` runs the loops above the cap, and `bounds_simd` covers the dev-mode check of a `load`.
