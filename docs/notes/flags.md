# Wrapping, saturating and flags

Choices made for the wrapping and saturating operators, `mul_high` and the
flags form in each pass. They describe the inside of the compiler.
`docs/decisions.md` holds what a reader of the language or a user of the
tools can observe, under "Wrapping and saturating operators and `Flags`".

- The lexer reads `+% -% *% <<% +| -| *|` as one token each by the longest match of its table. `TOKEN_MUL_HIGH` has a spelling and no category, so no source text lexes as it.
- The parser gives each new operator the precedence of the plain one it extends.
- The checker turns a call of `mul_high` into a binary expression of `TOKEN_MUL_HIGH` when no name in scope is `mul_high`. The operand rules, the constants and the lowering of an operator then hold for it. `src/antic/arith.c` computes the upper half of a product and a saturated result at 8, 16, 32 and 64 bits. The checker, the optimizer and the symbolic folder all call it, so a constant has one value in each.
- The checker counts the fields that a function reads of each Flags local or parameter in `flags_read` of its symbol. The base of the field being checked stands in `field_base`. The name there counts one field, and any other use of the name counts all four.
- `let (result, flags) = e;` whose value is an operator on an integer is the flags form. Its statement symbol holds the pair `(T, Flags)` for the dump and has no place. A flags name that assigns a variable in scope carries `assigns`, and the slots of the frame leave it alone.
- `a + f.carry` and `a - f.carry` carry `carry` on their node. A literal before the carry takes the type of the context rather than `bool`.
- Lowering writes the flags form as one flag operation, `addfl`, `subfl`, `mulfl`, `shlfl`, `sshrfl`, `ushrfl` or `negfl`, and one `flag` per bit of the mask. `a + b + f.carry` is `addfl a, b, c` with the bool as the third operand, and a carry after any other operand is `addfl x, 0, c`. The operands are lowered left to right and the carry last. A plain carry in of a signed type reads the overflow flag and branches to the dev-mode check, which the release build drops with its read.
- The verifier keeps the reads right after their operation, with other reads alone between, as it keeps `branchov` after its operation. The optimizer folds neither and treats both as pure, so an unread flag goes and an operation without a read or a use goes too.
- `src/antic/expand.c` runs in `select_module` after the layout, when every type has its width and `c_wchar` its signedness. It expands each saturating operation into the plain one, a test whether it left the range, and a mask that chooses the result or the bound. It expands each flag operation that the target does not select into the plain operation and a portable test per read: an unsigned comparison of the result with an operand for a carry, the sign of two exclusive-ors for an overflow, `mulh` for a product and a shift by the count less one for the last bit. The reads then write their temporaries, and the result goes to its own last, since it may be an operand.
- x86_64 selects `addfl`, `subfl` and `negfl` at every width as `add`, `sub` and `neg`, and with a carry in as `bt $0` on the bool and `adc` or `sbb`. Each read is a `set` right after them: `o`, `b`, `e` or `s`. ARM64 selects them at 32 and 64 bits as `adds`, `subs` and `negs`. A carry in takes bit 0 of the bool with `and`, then `cmp` of it with 1 sets C for `adcs`, and `negs` of it clears C for a borrow into `sbcs`. Each read is a `cset`, with `hs` for the carry of an add and `lo` for the borrow of a subtract. A narrower type on ARM64 and every other flag operation take the expansion.
- `mul_high` is `smulh` or `umulh` at 64 bits on ARM64 and the one-operand `imul` or `mul` on x86_64. A narrower product is exact in a register twice as wide, or in 32 bits, and its upper half is a shift away.
- The tests: `program_wrapping`, `program_saturating`, `program_mul_high`, `program_flags` and `program_carry_chain` run on ARM64 and, through Rosetta, on x86_64, and each in dev mode. `checks` holds `overflow_carry`. The unit tests of `test_x86_64.c` and `test_arm64.c` pin `adc`, `sbb`, `adcs` and `sbcs`, `test_lower.c` pins the one read of an unused flag, and `clib_flags` passes `Flags` to C and back.
