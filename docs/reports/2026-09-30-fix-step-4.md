# Fix step 4: antic parser and lexer

Step 4 of "Fix steps" in `docs/audit/summary.md`: S05, S08 and M37, from
`docs/audit/front-end.md` and `docs/audit/input-readers.md`. The logs lie
in `build/drive/logs/s4-*.log`.

## Findings

| Id | Result |
|---|---|
| S05 | Fixed, `925c240` |
| S08 | Fixed, `925c240` |
| M37 | Fixed, `925c240` |

S05. `descend` counted the parser's recursion and not the depth of the
tree. A chain built in a loop now counts one level per link through the
reach of the tree, in `binary`, `postfix` and `cast`, each wrapped by
`chain_begin` and `chain_end`. `nested_type` descends once per type, which
also bounds `push_item`. The scan of `<` counts a level per `scan_type`
and reads no list past `PARSE_DEPTH_MAX`. The lexer counts nested
`f"..."` and refuses one deeper than `LEX_FORMAT_DEPTH_MAX`. Before the
fix, seven chain forms at 200000 links parsed without a message, and
`class A {` 200000 times ended the unit tests with a segfault
(`s4-unit-prefix.log`). A literal nested 257 levels deep lexed without
an error (`s4-unit-unfixed.log`).

S08. `placeholder` gives the inner parser an origin map of its own, each
token its own origin. `for` and `assert` in a closure inside `f"..."`
read their source text through it. The test segfaulted on the unfixed
parser (`s4-unit-unfixed.log`) and now checks the text of the assert.

M37. `peek_at` walked from the parser's position on every call. It now
takes one step against the index of the last token, so every lookahead
and the scan read in constant time per token. With the scan's depth
bound, `a < a < ...` of 200000 names takes 0.21 s to refuse
(`s4-lt.log`). The finding's own fix was a scan with an index of its own.
The step fixes the cause for every caller of `peek_at` instead.

Truncated sources, cut inside the scan, inside an `{expr}` and inside a
nested class, are refused without a crash. That covers the parser part
of M43 named in the step.

## Provisional entries

- After the entry on `PARSE_DEPTH_MAX` in `docs/decisions.md`: the limit
  holds for the tree the parser builds, one level per link of a chain and
  per nested type. An `f"..."` nests at most 256 levels.
