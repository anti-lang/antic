# anti fmt

`anti fmt` writes the canonical form of "Formatter rules" in
`docs/tooling-addendum.md`. `std/` and `tests/` stand in that form, the
formatting class of `anti check` is the byte comparison the addendum names, and
`tools/scripts/format_anti.py`, which stood in for the command, is gone.

## What was built

- `tools/anti/fmt.c` and `tools/anti/fmt.h`, and the command `anti fmt` with
  `--check` and a list of files. Without a file it takes the source and the
  test directory of `[layout]`, as `anti check` does.
- The indent, one tab per level, with one more for a wrapped line and nothing
  aligned past it. An item body opens its brace on its own line and a statement
  block on the line of its statement. `} else {` and `} while cond` come to one
  line each. The parentheses around a whole condition go, and a statement takes
  a line of its own. A doc comment is filled to 80 columns and loses its ` * `
  gutter, and an ordinary comment stands where the author put it.
- The formatter reads the compiler's own lexer. The lexer drops an ordinary
  comment, so the bytes between two tokens are scanned for one and the two
  streams are merged. The line breaks of the author stand where no rule moves
  them, so no expression is re-flowed.
- The test `anti_fmt` reads `tests/fmt/loose.anti` against
  `tests/fmt/loose.expected`, runs the formatter over its own output and checks
  that `--check` writes nothing. That is the format stability of
  `docs/tooling.md`.
- The test `fmt_canonical` reads every source of `std/` and `tests/`. Seven
  files stand outside it. The layout or the lexical error of each is the point
  of the test it belongs to.
- The formatting class of `anti check` writes the canonical text with
  `fmt_source` and compares the bytes. `tools/anti/format.c`, which read the
  rules against the token stream, is gone.

229 files of `std/` and `tests/` were written in the canonical form.

## What failed and how it was fixed

The corpus was the oracle. Each run over a copy of `std/` and `tests/` showed
what the rules got wrong, and eight readings were corrected against it:

- `[]*Object` took a space after the `]`, because the `*` was read as a
  product. The `]` of a type now marks the `*` behind it as a pointer.
- `pub fn trace(...)` took a space before the `(`. The list of contextual words
  was cut to `in` and `by`, which are the two that can stand before a unary
  operator, and it no longer reaches a call or an index.
- `simd.select(m, a, b)` took a space, because `select` is a keyword. A name
  behind a dot now ends a value whatever the lexer calls the word.
- A one-line body inside a class took an extra tab, because the statement of an
  inlined item body stayed open behind it.
- `layer: u32 : 4` lost the space of the bitfield width. `x: 1, y: 2` then
  gained one, because the colons of a field were counted across a comma and
  across an inline brace.
- A bare block after another joined the line of its `}`.
- `for i, x in items` lost its `for`, because the comma reset the whole
  statement. The comma now ends the field, the arm or the case and leaves the
  word that governs.
- A blank line inside a block doc comment was dropped, because the flush of a
  line returned early on an empty one.

The suite then failed four tests, all of them line numbers that the re-wrapped
doc comments had moved: `warning_doc_markup`, the two `.err` files of
`deserialize_alloc` and `inject_type`, and `emit_identity`, whose manifest
carries the assembly of every program and therefore the line a check message
names. The three expectations were written again from the compiler's own
output, and the manifest with `-DWRITE=yes` on the Mac.

One defect was found by reading the listings of the specifications against the
formatter: a trailing `// comment` behind a `;` moved onto the next line,
because one statement per line ended the line at the semicolon. The line now
ends after the comment, and the fixture carries the case. No file of `std/` or
`tests/` held one, so nothing was written twice.

## The provisional entries

Eight entries went into `docs/decisions.md` under "The formatter", where the
rules were silent:

- The canonical form keeps the line breaks of the author and re-flows no
  expression.
- A body whose `}` stands on the line of its `{` keeps to that line. The braces
  of one that spans lines each take a line of their own.
- An item body is the body of `fn`, `struct`, `union`, `class`, `enum`,
  `variant`, `tests` and `fixtures`.
- A brace that no word of its statement governs builds a value. A brace with no
  statement before it opens a block of its own line.
- A run of empty lines becomes one.
- A space stands inside an empty brace and before the colon of a bitfield
  width.
- A doc comment is filled to 80 columns, where a tab counts four.
- A source the lexer refuses stays as it is.

The entry on the formatting class of `anti check` lost its `[provisional]` tag,
because it now does what it said it would do once `anti fmt` existed.
