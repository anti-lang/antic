# Fix step 18, `==` and hash in lowering

Step 18 of "Fix steps" in `docs/audit/summary.md`: M13 with one walk of
the parts, and the minor finding that copies `holds_union` into `types.c`.
The files are `lower_eq.c`, `lower_hash.c`, `sema_hash.c` and `types.c`.
The fix also needed `lower_lowerer.h` and `types.h`. Logs are in
`build/drive/logs/s18-*`.

Status: green and pushed.

## Findings

| ID | Result |
|---|---|
| M13 | Fixed. `lower_part_of` of `lower_eq.c` names the kind of a part, the operator the checker listed first. `compare_at` and `hash_at` each switch over every kind without a default case, so `-Wswitch` refuses a kind that one of them leaves out. `lower_part_scalar` reads a scalar for both, an `f16` as its `f32`. `lower_field_skipped` passes over a unit break, a lock and an array of locks in a struct for both. `lower_member_of` gives the form of a class field to `compare_member` and `hash_member`. `own_eq` and `own_hash` are gone. |
| Minor, `holds_union` copied | Fixed. `types_holds_union` of `types.c` replaces `lower_unreadable` of `lower_eq.c` and `holds_union` of `sema_hash.c`. It keeps the guard against a type met twice that the checker's copy had. |

The four drifts of M13, each closed by the one rule:

- A union with `operator fn eq` in a struct: the operator goes before the
  kind, so `compare_at` calls it.
- `f16`: compared through `lower_part_scalar`, so -0 and +0 are one value,
  as the hash already took them.
- A function with its context: compared and hashed by its code. The hash
  had taken both words.
- `[2]Mutex` in a class: a lock or an array of locks is `LOWER_PART_NONE`
  and is no part of the class for `lower_member_of`.

## Decisions

In `docs/decisions.md`:

- Added under "Files of lowering", `[provisional]`: the default `==` and
  hash take each part by `lower_part_of` and each class field by
  `lower_member_of`. An operator goes before the kind, an `f16` compares
  as its `f32`, and a lock or an array of locks is passed over.
- Changed under "Generics and collections", `[provisional]`: a function
  with its context hashes by its code, and an `own fn` field by its code
  and its snapshot. The entry said its two words. That went against the
  rule of "Hashing and order" in `docs/anti-language-additions.md`, that
  values equal by `eq` hash alike, and against the entries that compare a
  function by its code. The entry records the change and its reason.
- Changed under "Generics and collections", `[provisional]`: a slice
  inside a value hashes as the view `==` compares. Its elements count only
  where the slice is the value hashed. An array of slices and a `?T`
  of a slice hashed the elements before. That broke no rule, since equal
  views have equal elements, but it was a second rule for one part.

Question for Eddie: the change of the hash of a function with its context
is the smaller of two ways to make `==` and hash agree. The other is to
compare both words. That would change two entries, and `f == g` on two
closures with them.
