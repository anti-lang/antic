# Fix step 11: runtime integer bounds

Step 11 of "Fix steps" in `docs/audit/summary.md`. Files: `src/rt/text.c`,
`threads.c`, `toml.c`, `conf.c` with `conf.h`, and `atomic.c`. Logs are
under `build/drive/logs/`, named `s11-*`.

## Findings

- S26, fixed. `reserve` refuses a count past `INT64_MAX - 1 - length`,
  and the doubling stops before it passes `INT64_MAX / 2`. Before the
  fix the unit run died of SIGBUS in `anti_rt_builder_fill`
  (`s11-unit-red.log`). Tests: `builder_bounds` of
  `tests/unit/test_rt_bounds.c`.
- S27, fixed. `anti_rt_parallel` checks the product against the bound of
  `anti_rt_chan_new` and stops with a message. Before the fix the program
  of `tests/traps/parallel_size.anti` died of SIGBUS with no message
  (`s11-ps-red.log`). Test: `parallel_size`.
- S30, fixed. `repeats` gives -1 at `INT64_MAX` and the header is
  refused. Tests: `table_repeat_limit` of `tests/unit/test_toml.c`,
  which failed before the fix (`s11-unit-red.log`).
- S31, fixed. `anti_rt_conf_threads` of `conf.c` reads the digits
  against `INT32_MAX`, and `set_key` and `worker_count` both call it.
  Before the fix `--anti.threads=99999999999` ran (`s11-threads-red.log`).
  Tests: `conf_threads_range`, `conf_threads_option`,
  `conf_threads_zero`.
- S36, fixed. `anti_rt_atomic_sub` is `__atomic_fetch_sub` per width, and
  on MSVC an add of the unsigned negation. The old body aborted under
  UBSan at `INT64_MIN` (`s11-atomic-old.log`, `s11-atomic-new.log`).
  Tests: `atomic_sub_minimum` of `tests/unit/test_rt_bounds.c`. The unit
  tests now compile `src/rt/atomic.c`.
- S11, the precision in `toml.c`, fixed. `read_key` refuses a key of
  `PATH_ROOM` bytes or more, so `(int)` of its length is a valid
  precision. The defect needs a key of 2^31 bytes, which no suite test
  holds. `key_length` of `tests/unit/test_toml.c` checks the bound at
  319 and 320 bytes, bare, quoted and inside an inline table.
- M18, fixed. `add` frees both copies of a pair it does not count. No
  test shows it: the leak needs a failed `malloc`, and the unit tests
  have no way to make one fail.

## Other changes
