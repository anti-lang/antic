# The safety check use-after-free

The step built E2 of "Compile-time checks of 2026-10-20" in
`docs/decisions.md`. Logs are under `build/drive/logs/checks-e2/`.

## What changed

Commit `90810958` builds the check. `src/antic/sema_free.c` walks the
paths of every function body after the checker and after E1. A local or
a parameter dies on a path by `delete`, `destroy`, a move into an `own`
parameter, a literal or a local, and a store into an `own` field. An
`own` field of `self` dies by `delete`, `destroy` and a move. On a path
after the death the pass refuses a read, a field access, a call, an
index, a second `delete` or `destroy` and a move, until an assignment. A
join keeps a death of one path, and a loop body is walked twice for the
next turn. `=` to a dead `own` field of `self` tears nothing down: the
checker marks the statement, lowering reads the mark, and the library
file carries it, format version 89.

The tests came first and failed, `red.log` and `allowed-red.log`: 15
refusals in `tests/matrix/ownership_*`, one program of the allowed uses
and `tests/errors/use_after_free` for a dev build and `unchecked`. With
the mark of lowering turned off, the allowed program fails under
`--memory-checks` with a double free, `t-noflag.log`.

The pass replaces the order of the text the checker followed for moves.
Its messages gained the line of the move and the name of the check, in
28 expected outputs and in 8 refused blocks of the guide.
Three error files held a move beside other errors in one function. The
pass skips a function with an error of the checker, so those cases moved
into functions of their own. Commit `ecaeb5ae` corrects the lines
of `destroy_locals.err` after `anti fmt` wrapped its header.

Commit `18e6b908` marks the one place of the repository the check
refuses, with `unchecked(use-after-free, "until the step sweep")`.

## Mistakes of the task

- "Returning it" kills a value, but a `return` ends its path, so no use
  follows one. E2 has no message and no test for it.
- The rule refuses a read after `destroy`. `destroy(p)` and then
  `free(p)` is how a program ends memory of `alloc(T, n)` or of C: 24
  places in `tests/` and one in `anti.collection.concurrent` do it. The
  pass lets `free(p)` and a view as `*byte` pass after `destroy`.
- The guide and three entries described the order of the text: a move
  on each path of an `if` and `=` after a move were refused. E2, which
  stands higher, makes both legal. The guide, the specifications and a
  provisional entry follow E2.

## Question for Eddie

E2 counts a plain pointer stored into an `own` field as moved, so
`h.node = n; n.v = 1;` is refused although the object is alive. Should a
store into an `own` field move the pointer, or leave it readable?

## Provisional entries added

`own` field moves, `=` to a field dead on one path tears nothing down, a
death is refused once on a path, a `defer` or a closure naming a dead
value is refused, and E2 runs after E1 on a function without an error.
