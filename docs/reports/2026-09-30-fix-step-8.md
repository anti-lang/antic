# Fix step 8: antic optimizer and register allocator

Step 8 of "Fix steps" in `docs/audit/summary.md`: S17, S18, S21 with a
frame limit on both targets, and M53. The detail stands in
`docs/audit/back-end.md`. The logs lie in `build/drive/logs/s8-*.log`.

## Findings

| Id | Result |
|---|---|
| S17 | Fixed, `7dbcf4f` |
| S18 | Fixed, `8a2d002` |
| S21 | Fixed, `2b34092` |
| M53 | Fixed, `efb12d0` |

S17. `forward_stores` forgets the held address after a load whose
result names its base or its offset. `programs/forward_own_base.anti`
walks `p = p.link` and reads `p.link.v`. It printed 2 before the fix and
prints 3.

S18. `written_again` makes a slot escape when its own temporary or the
result of a ptradd of it has a second definition.
`programs/slot_two_addresses.anti` points `q` at a field of one slot and,
in a branch, at a field of another. It printed `1 1` and prints `2 1`.

S21. `layout_frame` checks each alignment and each sum against the limit
of the target before it makes it, and returns false past it. ARM64 takes
the limit of x86_64. `test_emit.c` holds eight slots of `2^61 - 1` bytes
on both targets, which x86_64 laid out in 48 bytes before, and the frame
of 3000000000 bytes on ARM64 as well. The message now reads "needs more
than 2147483647 bytes", since a frame past the limit may have no size
that fits 64 bits.

M53. `read_vregs` names every virtual register an operand reads, the
base and the index of a memory operand among them, and the rewrite of
`rematerialise_constants` goes through it. `constant_in_address` of
`test_regalloc.c` builds IR that skips the optimizer and loads through a
constant base and a constant index. Both registers were left without a
definition before on ARM64 and on x86_64.

No output of an existing program changed. The two new programs added
twelve lines to `tests/emit-identity/programs.sha256`.

## Provisional entries

- ARM64 frames of `2^31` bytes or more are a compile error, and frame
  layout checks each sum against the limit. Under "Compiler behaviour"
  in `docs/decisions.md`, beside the entry of x86_64.
