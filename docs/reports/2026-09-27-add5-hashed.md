# The hashed maps and sets, second session

The first session, `docs/reports/2026-09-25-add5-hashed.md`, built the maps and
sets and stopped on four points. Main has since resolved three of them, and the
branch was rebased onto `d8865cd`. This session builds what those three
allowed. The fourth still blocks.

## What was done

- `union(o)` on `Set`, `HashSet` and `BitSet`, since `union` names a member
  after `fn` and a dot. A `Set` gives its own elements in order and then the
  new ones of `o`.
- `==` of `HashMap` and `HashSet`, an `operator fn eq` of each module beside
  the one of `Map` and `Set`, under the shared names `eq:HashMap` and
  `eq:HashSet`. A hashed `==` compares the entries in any order.
- The tests `std_map`, `std_hash_map`, `std_set` and `std_hash_set` and their
  `_dev` runs call `==` and `union`, since `==` of a generic class now reaches
  a program from a library file. `==` over maps of owning keys and values runs
  under the block count and ends with no block alive.
- `docs/decisions-hashed.md` records the two new provisional entries and
  lists the three resolved points apart from the one still blocked.

## What failed and how it was fixed

- `*w == *v` in the hashed `==`: `for (k, v) in m` gives a copy of the value,
  not a pointer. It compares `*w == v`.
- A `while` that passed `i` to `add(own x)` was refused as a move inside a
  loop. The test uses `for i in 1..9 by -1`.
- `==` of two maps of `Owned` values gave `false` for a copy, which is right:
  the default `==` of `Owned` compares its pointer. The test uses `Id`, which
  has `operator fn eq`.
- `fmt_canonical` and `anti_check` found one comment of `set.anti` wrapped
  short. `anti fmt` rewrapped it.
- `release_dry_run` failed once in the first full run, after 0.49 seconds, and
  passed alone and in the second full run. The step changes nothing it reads.

## Provisional decisions

- `==` of `HashMap` and `HashSet` holds when the counts agree and the other
  holds every entry, in any order.
- `union` of `Set` keeps the order of this set and then of the other, and
  every set operation takes the allocator of this set.

## Blocked

- `serialize` of a `HashMap` writes a key of a struct, a class or a variant
  with `operator fn lt` in slot order. A generic cannot ask whether its
  argument has `lt`, so sorting such a key needs a compile-time test of a hook
  in `src/antic/`, which lies outside this step, or a decision on another form.
  Keys of the built-in types sort.
