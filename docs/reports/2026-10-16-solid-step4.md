# Solid: step 4, patterns and the readers of text

The step fixed the findings of step 4 of "Fix steps" in
`docs/audit/summary.md`: S324, S330 and M313, with the minor findings of
`regex.c` and `toml.c` under rules 5, 12 and 14. It ran in one session on

| S330 | `e4688507` | `anti_rt_regex_patch` walked the matches a second time to write, and that walk searched the data it was writing, which a lookahead group or a lookbehind reads. |

| Rule 12, `regex.c:491` | `0d2a7566` | `anti_rt_regex_compile_bytes` freed its two blocks before each of three returns. |
| Rule 5, `toml.c:104` | `6ec49dc0` | The room of the pairs of a TOML document doubled through an unchecked product. |
| Rule 14, `toml.c:139` | `b71e491e` | `repeats` walked every pair read so far for each `[[name]]` header. |

`8c3718e8` writes the commit of each into its row of the summary.

- S324. `unit_json` skips the refused numbers alone, in an object and in
  an array, and asks `anti_rt_json_member` for one. `std_json_strings`
  asks `json.member` for five. Before the fix both tests failed.
- S330. `program_pattern_lookaround` patches `x(?=(y+))` over `xyyyy`
  and `(?<=a)(b)` over `abb`. Before the fix the first failed with
  `LengthMismatch` with the data changed and the second patched two
  places. One walk now records where `with` goes, and the writes follow.
- M313. `unit_regex` compiles every prefix of one byte pattern that holds
  each construct the rewriter reads ahead in, each a heap copy without a
  NUL. It passes in the host and the asan trees, as the audit's reading
  found no defect. S304 and S305, which M313 names too, take their tests
  in step 12.
- Rule 14 at `regex.c:578`. `unit_regex` asks for the group `a`, a NUL
  and `b`, which found the group `a`. The `antl_tree.c:1459` half of the
  finding belongs to step 12.
- Rule 12. No path leaked. `unit_regex` and the programs of byte patterns
  take the three paths.
- Rule 5. The count of pairs stays below the length of the input. No
  input reaches the overflow on a target of 64 bits, so no test can show
  it. A room whose double does not fit fails as memory that ran out, as
  `list_add` of `jsontree.c` does.
- Rule 14 at `toml.c:139`. `table_repeat_cost` of `unit_toml` reads
  100000 tables of one name and 100000 of distinct names. Each read took
  about 30 s of the processor before the fix, and the test asks for
  under 2 s. The paths of the keys are indexed once, at the first header
  after them. The count still follows the largest number under the name,
  which `table_repeat_limit` pins.
